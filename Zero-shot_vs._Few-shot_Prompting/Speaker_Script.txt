## Slide 3 – Core Concepts  

### 1️⃣ Zero‑shot Prompting  
- **Definition:** The model receives **only the task description** (and perhaps a question) – no examples.  
- **Analogy:** Imagine a Bollywood hero stepping onto the set **without a rehearsal**. He’s given the *script line* (“Save the kingdom!”) and must act immediately.  
- **When it shines:**  
  - When you have *no labeled examples* or the cost of curating examples is high.  
  - For *quick prototyping* or when the model has already seen similar tasks during pre‑training.  

### 2️⃣ Few‑shot Prompting  
- **Definition:** The prompt includes **a handful (1‑N) of task‑specific examples** before the new query.  
- **Analogy:** Our hero now has a *training montage*—a quick 3‑minute sequence showing him dodging bullets, climbing cliffs, and delivering a punchline. Those snapshots guide his performance on the final scene.  
- **When it shines:**  
  - When the task is *niche* or deviates from the model’s pre‑training distribution.  
  - When you can afford a few curated examples to boost accuracy.  

### 3️⃣ Prompt Anatomy (Both Styles)  
| Component | Zero‑shot | Few‑shot |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Task Description** | ✔︎ Required | ✔︎ Required |
| **Demonstration Examples** | ❌ Omitted | ✔︎ 1‑N examples |
| **Query / Input** | ✔︎ Appears after description | ✔︎ Appears after examples |
| **Length** | Typically shorter | Slightly longer (but still compact) |

## Slide 4 – Worked Example  

**Task:** *Translate English to Hindi*  

### Zero‑shot Prompt  

```
Translate the following sentence to Hindi:
"The sunset painted the sky in gold."
```

*What the model does:* It leans on its pre‑training knowledge of English‑Hindi pairs and generates the translation directly.  

### Few‑shot Prompt (3‑shot)  

```
Translate English to Hindi.

Example 1:
English: "I love mangoes."
Hindi: "Mujhe aam pasand hain."

Example 2:
English: "The train arrived on time."
Hindi: "Railgaadi samay par pahunchi."

Example 3:
English: "She sings beautifully."
Hindi: "Woh sundar gaati hai."

Now translate:
"The sunset painted the sky in gold."
```

**Break‑down (spoken cue):**  
- *[Enthusiastic tone]* “Notice how we gave the model three mini‑scenes—each a crisp, parallel pair. This is our ‘training montage’. The model now knows the *style* and *tone* we expect.”  

**Result comparison (quick on‑screen table):**  

| Prompt Type | Typical BLEU Score (sample) | Latency |
|-------------|----------------------------|---------|
| Zero‑shot   | 28‑30                       | ~0.5 s |
| Few‑shot (3) | 35‑38                       | ~0.7 s |

## Slide 5 – Comparison: Zero‑shot vs. Few‑shot  

| Dimension | Zero‑shot | Few‑shot |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Data Requirement** | None (besides task description) | 1‑N examples (usually 1‑5) |
| **Prompt Length** | Short → Faster inference | Longer → Slightly slower |
| **Accuracy** | Good for *in‑distribution* tasks | Better for *out‑of‑distribution* or nuanced tasks |
| **Robustness to Prompt Variations** | Sensitive to wording of the description | Gains stability from examples |
| **Cost of Preparation** | Minimal | Small labeling effort |
| **Best Use‑Case Metaphor** | Hero walking onto set with only the director’s cue | Hero doing a quick *training montage* before the climax |

**Cinematic takeaway:** Zero‑shot is the *“one‑take”* stunt—high risk, high speed. Few‑shot is the *“slow‑mo montage”*—more preparation, higher chance of a perfect landing.

## Slide 6 – Recap  

1. **Zero‑shot** = task description only; think “hero, no rehearsal”.  
2. **Few‑shot** = description + a few examples; think “hero’s training montage”.  
3. **When to pick:**  
   - *Zero‑shot* for rapid prototypes, abundant pre‑training knowledge, or when labeling is impossible.  
   - *Few‑shot* when you need that extra accuracy boost and can spare a few curated pairs.  
4. **Prompt design matters:** clear instruction, consistent formatting, and minimal ambiguity are the “director’s vision” that guides the LLM.

## Slide 7 – Knowledge Check (Mini‑Quiz)  

**1️⃣** *True or False:* Zero‑shot prompting always yields higher latency than few‑shot prompting.  

**2️⃣** In a few‑shot prompt, why is it important to keep the **example format** consistent?  

**3️⃣** You have a brand‑new, domain‑specific FAQ dataset (only 10 questions). Which prompting style would you start with and why?  

*(Pause for audience to think – “Dramatic interval” cue)*  

**Answers (reveal on next slide or handout):**  
1. **False** – few‑shot prompts are usually slightly longer, so latency can be a bit higher.  
2. Consistency signals the pattern the model should follow; inconsistent formatting confuses the “script”.  
3. Start with **few‑shot** – the handful of examples give the model the domain context it otherwise lacks.
